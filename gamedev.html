Platforms, Player Modes, Time Intervals, Goals, and Game Genres

1. Game Platforms

Arcade

Stand-alone systems in public venues (malls/parks). Usually played standing up using buttons and joysticks.

Console

Home systems hooked to a TV. Proprietary platforms controlled by manufacturers (Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo).

Computer

Played on PCs/laptops. Adds complexity due to non-standard setups and hardware variety.

Online

Played via Internet-connected PC or console. Large games use multiple servers for thousands of players.

Handheld

Lightweight, portable system with built-in screen, controls, and speakers (playable almost anywhere).

Mobile

Designed for smartphones/tablets. Has wide connectivity features for wireless multiplayer play.

Tabletop

Played on tables using physical pieces: board, card, dice, and pen-and-paper games (e.g., Dungeons & Dragons).

2. Time Intervals & Pacing

Turn-Based

Players take discrete turns with usually unlimited time. Encourages reflective planning (e.g., board/card games).

Real-Time

No turns or time intervals between moves. High-speed, reflexive gameplay; popular in online multiplayer.

Time-Limited

Compromise between turn-based and real-time. Sets a strict countdown for individual turns or entire puzzle sessions.

3. Player Modes

Single-Player

1 human player. Opponents or allies are AI characters / Non-Player Characters (NPCs).

Two-Player

Head-to-head mode. 2 players play against each other or team up against common enemies.

Local Multiplayer

Up to 4 players share the same physical space, same machine, and same screen with separate controllers.

LAN-Based Multiplayer

Players connect via a Local Area Network. Separate screens/devices, same physical local area.

Online Multiplayer

Players connect via the Internet across separate locations. Can support thousands of players at once.

MMORPG Key Feature

Persists 24/7 online and typically uses a subscription-based monthly fee model.

4. Game Development Goals

Entertainment

Pure fun, stress relief, boredom alleviation, role-play, and problem-solving experience.

Social

Facilitates social interaction between opponents or teammates (from 2 players to thousands in MMOs).

Educational

Teaches through in-game knowledge acquisition (math, geography, reading, etc.).

Recruitment & Training

Used by military, government (NASA/Air Force), and corporations for skills, navigation, and leadership training.

Health & Fitness

Exergames (Wii Fit, Just Dance) and medical therapy (e.g., Re-Mission for cancer patients).

Consciousness & Change

Raises awareness for social, political, or religious causes (poverty, climate change, human rights).

Aesthetics & Creativity

Elicits creative expression or artistic sharing (e.g., WarioWare D.I.Y., Guitar Hero GHTunes).

Marketing & Advertising

Includes Advergames (standalone brand ads) and Advertainment (branded interactive hangout sites).

5. Primary Game Genres & Sub-genres

Action

Focuses on player reaction time, reflex over reflection, snap judgments, and adrenaline rush.

  • Platformer: Dodge obstacles, jumping, item collection.
  • Shooter: Combat via guns/weapons (FPS = 1st person straight vision; 3rd person = wide perspective).
  • Racing: High-speed vehicle navigation on tracks.
  • Fighting: 1-on-1 side-view combat, average session ~90 secs.

Adventure & Hybrids

Storytelling, exploration, puzzle solving, and decoding. Usually turn-based/reflective.

  • Action-Adventure: Only hybrid genre universally recognized as its own distinct category. Blends fast reflex combat with story/puzzles.

Puzzle & Casino

Puzzle: Solving puzzles without controlling a character, little/no story (e.g., Tetris).

Casino: Electronic versions of real gambling (poker, slots) driven by risk/addiction motivations.

Role-Playing Games (RPGs)

Strong storylines + character progression (gaining XP, money, strength, equipment). Common theme: "Save the world".

Simulations (Sims)

Replicates real-world systems and machines.

  • Vehicle Sims: Complex machinery/flight training.
  • Sports/Participatory Sims: Wish fulfillment, playing/coaching sports.
  • Process Sims (CMS): Constructive building/management (no enemy destruction).

Strategy & MMOs

Strategy: Derived from board games (chess). Managing limited resources (TBS = reflective decisions; RTS = time pressure & micromanagement/multitasking).

MMO: Massive online worlds (MMORPG, MMOFPS, MMORTS). Challenge: Player misbehavior/harassment.
